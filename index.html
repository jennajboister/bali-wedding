Before you fly

things to sort out: Visas & entry

Passport holders from Australia, New Zealand, the UK and Europe need all three of these to get through Ngurah Rai Airport smoothly. Each person, including children, needs their own. Tick them off as you go; your ticks are saved on this device only.

  1. 0

    Check your passport

    It should be valid for at least six months after the day you arrive and have blank pages. Renewals can take weeks, so check now. Bali is strict about damaged passports: even a small tear or water damage can get you turned away at the airport, so if yours is looking worn, renew it before you travel.

    Freeif it's current
  2. 1

    Visa on Arrival (apply online as an e-VOA)

    A 30-day tourist visa. Apply online before you leave through the official portal at evisa.imigrasi.go.id. With an e-passport and an approved e-VOA you can use the automatic e-gates and skip the visa queue. Children 6 years of age or older with an eligible biometric e-passport and a pre-paid visa can use the e-gates. For children under 6 years you will need to queue even with an e-visa. You can also buy a visa on arrival at the airport, but expect a wait.

    IDR 500,000about A$50 per person
  3. 2

    All Indonesia arrival card

    A free online form that covers immigration, customs and health. Fill it in within the 72 hours before you land, at allindonesia.imigrasi.go.id.

    Freenever pay for it
  4. 3

    Bali tourist levy

    A one-off payment per person per trip, children included. Pay ahead on the official Love Bali site at lovebali.baliprov.go.id and save the QR voucher on your phone.

    IDR 150,000about A$15 per person

Use only the official sites linked above. Lookalike websites charge extra fees for the free arrival card and the fixed-price levy. Fees were correct as of October 2026, and Indonesia has floated both raising the visa fee and making it free for Australians, so check Smartraveller a few weeks before you go.

What to pack

Weather in July

July is the middle of Bali's dry season, and one of the best months to visit: sunny days, low humidity and very little rain. It's also school-holiday season for Australians, so book flights and rooms early.

Evenings~23°C

Pack

  •  Sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses
  • Insect repellent for the evenings
  • A light jacket, cardigan or wrap for evenings which get cool at night
  • Thongs/sandals

Times

Bali is on Central Indonesia Time (UTC+8), two hours behind Sydney in July. Sunset is a little after 6 pm.

Good to know

Getting there & getting around

Flights

Fly into Ngurah Rai International Airport (DPS), also called Denpasar. Direct flights from Sydney take around six and a half hours, and there are direct options from most Australian capitals.

Getting around

Ride-hailing apps such as Grab and Gojek work well and are cheap. For day trips, hiring a private driver for the day is common and good value. Allow plenty of time: traffic in the south is often slow.

We also have the contact details for a number of reliable drivers, which we'll share closer to the time.

Money

The currency is the Indonesian rupiah (IDR). Cards are fine at hotels and restaurants, but carry some cash for drivers, markets and tips. Use ATMs inside banks or shopping centres and count your change at money changers.

Health & insurance

Travel insurance is a must; check it covers medical evacuation and any activities like scooters or surfing. Drink bottled or filtered water.

Phones

Buy a local SIM or an eSIM on arrival or before you fly; it's far cheaper than roaming. Most people in Indonesia use WhatsApp, so have it installed.

Little travellers

What to know if travelling to Bali with kids

Bali is a great place for children, and Nusa Dua is one of the calmest, most family-friendly parts of the island. A bit of preparation makes the trip much easier.

Passports & paperwork

  • Every child needs their own passport, valid for at least six months after you arrive and in good condition.
  • Each child needs their own visa, as well as their own tourist levy and arrival card.
  • Children aged 6 and over can use the e-gates with an e-passport and e-VOA. Younger children go through the staffed queue.
  • If only one parent is travelling, a signed consent letter from the other parent can save questions at the airport.

Health before you go

  • Pack any regular medicines with a copy of the prescription and a doctor's letter. Some ADHD medications are illegal in Indonesia, so check before you travel.
  • Get travel insurance that covers every child, including medical evacuation. BIMC Hospital in Nusa Dua treats international patients.

Food, water & mosquitoes

  • Use bottled or filtered water for drinking and brushing teeth.
  • Stick to busy places for food, and pack rehydration sachets in case of an upset tummy.
  • Dengue is spread by mosquitoes. Use a child-safe repellent, especially at dawn and dusk, and dress kids in light long sleeves in the evening.

Getting around & gear

  • Kids don't need car seats when travelling in Bali, but you can hire drivers who have them. Just ask when you book.
  • Footpaths are often uneven, so a baby carrier can be easier than a pram.
  • Nappies, wipes and snacks are easy to buy at large supermarkets, but bring your usual formula.
  • Many Nusa Dua resorts have kids' clubs and can arrange babysitting, so ask when you book.
